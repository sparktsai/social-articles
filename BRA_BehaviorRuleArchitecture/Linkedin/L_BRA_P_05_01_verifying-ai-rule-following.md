# LBRA-05 — How Do You Know the AI Actually Followed the Rule?

## Problem

Better output does not prove that a Rule was followed.
A Rule loaded into context, a passing test suite, or an unchanged file can all look reassuring while still leaving the actual development decision invisible.

## Outline

This post connects reusable Rules to Decision Evidence.
It explains why governance needs visibility into decisions, applicable constraints, candidate options, selected rationale, and impact.
It then shows why risk-triggered review and Rule PDCA are needed to make Rule governance practical instead of overwhelming.

## Brief

Using Rules improved the development process, but subjective improvement was not enough to prove compliance.
The missing piece was observability: preserving not only the final artifact, but also the decision behavior that produced it.
Decision Evidence can record context, inputs, constraints, candidate options, selected decisions, rationale, impact, traceability, and applicable Rules.
However, recording every decision creates a review-volume problem, because governance cannot manually inspect everything.
Decision Risk provides a reason to locate and review the evidence that matters most.
Once evidence, risk signals, review, and Rule revision connect through Rule identity and version, the Rule can enter a real PDCA cycle.

## Conclusion

A reusable Rule is not automatically a governed Rule.
It becomes governable when actual development behavior can be observed, reviewed, and used to improve the Rule over time.
