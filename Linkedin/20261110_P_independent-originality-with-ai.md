**In the GenAI era, even when a human proposes the concept and makes the decisions, how can they substantiate its originality?**

Imagine two researchers independently interpreting complex systems through the same biological lens.

Researcher A sees an enterprise as a human body. Its business systems form a nervous system, while each processing point in an enterprise workflow resembles a neuron receiving, transforming, and transmitting information.

Researcher B sees an AI agent in a similar way. Each node in an agentic workflow resembles a neuron, while execution emerges as state, instructions, and tool results move between nodes—like bioelectric activity propagating through a nervous system.

One begins with organizational design. The other begins with AI architecture.

They never read each other's work. Yet they arrive at the same underlying analogy.

The analogy is structural, not biological. What converges is their way of understanding connected nodes, signal transmission, and emergent behavior.

Did one copy the other? Can both still be original?

Now add another possibility: they used different AI models.

The models came from different providers and produced different responses. But suppose both large models had been trained on material drawn from the same data lake—one containing a body of knowledge that was especially popular when their training data was assembled.

The researchers did not share their work. The models were not the same. Yet a common upstream source could still make the same analogy more likely to appear in both conversations.

Generative AI brings this old philosophical problem into everyday professional work.

Two researchers can independently define different problems, reject alternatives, and make their own final decisions. Yet their choice environments may still contain overlapping language, associations, and conceptual paths—even when they use different models.

That does not make their judgment unreal. But it means we should separate three claims that are often treated as one:

**Human-led development**

**Independent conceptual origin**

**Absence of AI influence**

Evidence for one does not automatically establish the others.

A prompt history can show how a person contributed. A timestamp can establish chronology. A hash can preserve artifact integrity.

None can reveal the complete ancestry of an idea.

My latest article explores what independent originality can mean when AI is not merely a tool, but also a source of alternatives—and why evidence of human contribution is not necessarily proof of an idea's complete origin.

**If two people use different models, work in different fields, reach the same underlying concept, and develop different applications, how would you preserve the evidence of each person's contribution?**

Would you retain the complete conversation records, the version-by-version history of the work, and the exact model and model version used at each stage?

And what could those records establish—human contribution, chronology, independent development, or originality itself?
