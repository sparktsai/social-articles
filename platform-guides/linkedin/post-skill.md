# Write LinkedIn Posts

Create a publish-ready LinkedIn Post that gives one identifiable professional audience one credible and useful idea.

## Required reference

Read [post-guides.md](post-guides.md) completely before drafting or reviewing. Treat it as the authoritative platform, pattern, distribution, paid amplification, analytics, and integrity guide.

If current Post limits, Premium, Boost, advertising, media, or analytics behavior affects the task, verify it against official LinkedIn sources. Separate organic and paid distribution. Never promise impressions, recommendations, followers, leads, or business outcomes.

## Establish authorship

LinkedIn discourages generic, repetitive, recycled, low-substance AI slop. Require a real contribution from the user or source material:

- first-hand professional observation or experience;
- a concrete case, result, artifact, or failure;
- an original distinction, claim, framework, or boundary;
- a source-grounded professional implication;
- a verified checklist, tutorial, or decision method.

If none exists, provide focused questions, an outline, or explicit placeholders instead of fabricating professional experience.

Never invent roles, employers, projects, teams, customers, metrics, experiments, quotations, results, or credentials.

## Gather the brief

```text
Topic:
Target professional role:
Work situation:
Observation or evidence:
Core point:
Desired professional response:
Language and voice:
Format:
Character or media constraint:
Link, Article, or Newsletter relationship:
Organic or paid objective:
Output location:
```

Infer missing fields when safe. Ask only when an answer materially changes the Post or authorship evidence is missing.

## Distill the idea

Internally define:

1. **Professional** — who faces this situation?
2. **Situation** — what recognizable work context creates relevance?
3. **Tension** — what assumption, risk, boundary, or ownership gap matters?
4. **Point** — what one judgment should remain?
5. **Support** — what makes it credible?
6. **Response** — what useful discussion or action should follow?

Split independent claims into separate Posts or an Article.

## Route to a pattern

Choose by rhetorical job, then consult the full definitions in [post-guides.md](post-guides.md).

| Rhetorical job | Preferred pattern |
| --- | --- |
| Correct a professional misconception | 1. Professional myth → actual distinction |
| Turn a field observation into a broader lesson | 2. Field observation → larger mechanism |
| Replace a symptom with the real decision | 3. Surface problem → actual decision |
| Expose responsibility and verification gaps | 4. Tool capability → ownership |
| Move debate from ability to control | 5. Capability → boundary |
| Share a genuine change after building or testing | 6. Before／after viewpoint change |
| Extract what transfers from a case | 7. Case → transferable lesson |
| Separate concepts incorrectly treated as one | 8. A, B, and C differ |
| Give a practical readiness test | 9. Checklist／diagnostic |
| Use history to clarify a current decision | 10. Historical evolution → current judgment |
| Add conditions to oversimplified advice | 11. Common advice → missing condition |
| Promote long-form content with independent value | 12. Article／Newsletter launch |

Apply this process:

1. Identify the primary rhetorical job.
2. Choose one pattern; add a second only for a necessary logical step.
3. Reject any pattern that requires unsupported experience, data, alternatives, or certainty.
4. Rewrite in the author's natural voice; never expose template wording.
5. Keep the version that establishes the professional value with the least unnecessary context.

## Choose the format

| Content need | Preferred format |
| --- | --- |
| One argument or reflection | Text Post |
| Diagram, evidence, or comparison | Single image |
| Several related visual examples | Multi-image |
| Framework, checklist, or sequential visual explanation | Document Post |
| Demonstration or personal explanation with motion | Video |
| Meaningful choices with real trade-offs | Poll |
| External resource with an original takeaway | Link Post |
| Commentary on existing LinkedIn content | Repost with thoughts |
| Complete mechanism, multiple sections, or extensive evidence | Article |

Default to a Post only when the idea fits clearly within LinkedIn's current 3,000-character limit. Do not compress away evidence or boundaries merely to avoid using an Article.

## Draft the Post

### Opening

Use the first few lines to establish a specific work situation, tension, or conclusion. Make relevance visible to the target professional before asking them to expand the Post.

Avoid generic trend language, empty announcements, fake confessions, unsupported numbers, outrage hooks, and text designed only to trigger “See more.”

### Body

Develop one movement:

```text
Situation → Tension → Evidence or mechanism → Professional implication
```

Connect abstract ideas to roles, decisions, artifacts, ownership, acceptance criteria, evidence, and consequences. Preserve enough context for an out-of-network reader.

Use short paragraphs and intentional line breaks without turning every sentence into a dramatic fragment.

### Ending

Choose one:

- a decision takeaway;
- a practical diagnostic;
- a meaningful trade-off;
- a specific request for cases or counterexamples;
- a natural reason to read an Article or Newsletter.

Do not end with generic “Thoughts?”, engagement bait, comment-for-resource schemes, or requests to Like／Repost.

### Media

When media is included, provide a concise brief, caption, credit, ALT text, subtitle, or AI disclosure as applicable. The Post text must explain the takeaway rather than requiring readers to decode the media.

## Adapt source material

When adapting an Article, Newsletter, Medium story, X Post, notes, or transcript:

1. Extract one claim, case, framework, failure mode, or diagnostic.
2. Identify the LinkedIn professional role and decision.
3. Add only the context needed for independent understanding.
4. Preserve actual evidence and author voice.
5. Avoid repeating launch copy across multiple Posts.
6. Give every adaptation independent value before linking.

Do not summarize an entire Article into a crowded Post. Create a series of distinct angles when needed.

## Create variants only when useful

When requested, create 2–4 strategically different variants, such as:

- distinction-led;
- field-observation-led;
- diagnostic-led;
- case-led.

Do not produce superficial synonym changes. Label the difference briefly and recommend the strongest option when context allows.

## Prepare paid amplification only when requested

For Boost or Thought Leader Ads, add a separate note containing:

- objective;
- target role, industry, seniority, geography, and language;
- eligible content format;
- audience promise;
- organic baseline metrics;
- paid success metrics;
- disclosure or permission requirements.

Do not optimize every organic Post as ad copy. Never merge paid impressions with organic performance claims.

## Review gates

### Professional value

- The target role and work situation are recognizable.
- The Post delivers one useful distinction, observation, case, method, or question.
- The implication changes a decision, risk, ownership, or practice.
- An out-of-network reader can understand it independently.

### Authorship and integrity

- The Post reflects real author perspective, experience, or source-grounded analysis.
- It does not fabricate professional experience or evidence.
- It is not generic, repetitive, recycled, or formulaic AI content.
- Factual claims and current platform behavior are verified.
- Heavy AI assistance is disclosed when appropriate.

### Readability and format

- The opening reaches the subject quickly.
- Every paragraph advances the point.
- Line breaks improve reading rather than simulate drama.
- The content fits the 3,000-character limit or intentionally routes to Article.
- Media adds information value and accessibility support.
- Poll options represent meaningful alternatives.

### Conversation and distribution

- Any question invites specific professional experience or reasoning.
- The Post gives value before requesting a click or response.
- Mentions and hashtags are genuinely relevant.
- It is not identical or near-identical to recent planned content.
- Organic and paid objectives are separated.
- No wording promises impressions, recommendations, followers, leads, or outcomes.

## Deliver the result

Unless another format is requested, return only the publish-ready copy in a fenced `text` block.

When media or publishing assets were requested, place them after the copy under clearly labeled headings. When sources were used, list direct links under `Sources` outside the Post copy.

Do not expose internal planning notes unless requested. If author contribution or evidence is missing, return focused questions or a structured draft with explicit placeholders.

If writing to this repository, follow its existing LinkedIn platform, series, and filename conventions. Preserve unrelated working-tree changes.
