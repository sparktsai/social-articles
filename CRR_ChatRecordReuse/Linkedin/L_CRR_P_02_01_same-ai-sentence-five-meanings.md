An AI answer gets copied into a decision document.

Later, nobody remembers whether it was a verified finding, a recommendation, or merely an idea that still needed checking.

The words survived. Their meaning did not.

Suppose AI says:

> “This approach has three major risks.”

In a Learning conversation, those risks are concepts the Human needs to understand.

In Analysis, they are factors in a reasoned judgment.

In Advisory, they may support a recommendation.

In Review, they may be findings against defined expectations.

In Assistance, they may only be research leads that still require evidence.

Same sentence. Different status. Different next step.

AI conversations are not only Q&A. We also use AI to learn, advise, assist, discuss, explore, analyze, create, critique, review, challenge, and reflect.

Each relationship produces different reusable material:

- Inquiry can become a FAQ.
- Learning can become study notes.
- Advisory can support a decision record.
- Assistance can become a verification trail.
- Analysis can become a comparison model.
- Creation can become a formal artifact.
- Review can become issues and status.
- Reflection can become a retrospective.

Putting all of these under the single label “chat” hides the difference. It also makes it too easy to promote fluent AI output into evidence, approval, or fact.

Before reusing an AI response, ask:

1. What roles were Human and AI playing?
2. What was this interaction trying to produce?
3. Which parts are facts, assumptions, recommendations, findings, or unverified leads?
4. What should this material become next?

The purpose is not to label every message. It is to preserve the status of the content when it moves into another artifact.

How do you prevent AI suggestions from losing their original context when your team reuses them?
