Your AI archive keeps growing.

But finding anything worth reusing keeps getting harder.

Saving every conversation feels safe. In practice, a complete transcript can preserve everything and still preserve nothing clearly enough to use.

One long chat may contain the original question, unrelated troubleshooting, three new ideas, a completed decision, an unfinished review, and several unverified AI suggestions.

Exporting all of it preserves the mixing.

At the same time, not every AI chat deserves a permanent record. A one-line translation, syntax reminder, or temporary status check may be easier to request again.

The useful question is not:

> Was this conversation long?

It is:

> Would this conversation be expensive to reconstruct?

A Topic is usually worth preserving when it will continue, the background is costly to repeat, conclusions depend on assumptions, important claims remain unverified, or the material can support a future decision, issue, note, article, or workflow.

Then choose the recording boundary.

Use a full task export when the entire session belongs to one Topic and its surrounding context matters.

Use focused capture when only a bounded part belongs to the Topic. Start before that discussion and stop when it ends.

Focused capture reduces noise, but it still needs Human judgment. Start too late and necessary context disappears. Stop too late and unrelated work enters the record.

Before recording, define:

```text
Topic
Purpose
Expected output
Boundary
Stop condition
```

Length is a poor signal. A two-message decision may matter for years. A hundred-message brainstorm may be disposable.

The goal is not to remember every message. It is to preserve the part that still has a future.

How do you decide which AI conversations to keep—and where the useful boundary ends?
