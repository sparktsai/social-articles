# Thread: Scope, Boundary, Evidence

## Post 1 — Start a new Post

The same prompt changed different parts of the same specification or code.

Not different wording. Not naming style. Different scope.

I ran the prompt 10 times, then tried it across models.

That experiment changed how I thought about AI reliability and governance.

↓

---

## Post 2 — Reply to Post 1

I began picturing a prompt as light passing through layers of filters.

The signal follows one probable path and forms an answer. Send it again, and the path may be similar but not identical.

Not a model explanation. Just the mental picture behind my next experiment.

↓

---

## Post 3 — Reply to Post 2

My next step was simple:

Before asking AI to make a change, I recorded what I expected.

Then I compared that expectation with the actual change.

This moved the problem from “Was the answer good?” to “Did the change stay where I intended?”

↓

---

## Post 4 — Reply to Post 3

I added the expected area of change to the prompt, along with what should remain untouched.

The changes became more contained.

That was when Scope and Boundary stopped being abstract terms:

Scope said where to act.
Boundary said where to stop.

↓

---

## Post 5 — Reply to Post 4

Then I asked AI why it had made a particular change.

The answer was not convincing, so I asked the same question five times.

I received five different explanations.

The output alone could not give me a stable basis for reviewing the decision.

↓

---

## Post 6 — Reply to Post 5

I changed when I asked for the explanation.

Instead of asking afterward, I requested reasons while AI made the change:

• Why this choice?
• What were the alternatives?
• Why were they not selected?

The answers became stable enough to compare.

↓

---

## Post 7 — Reply to Post 6

Those answers were not proof of a model's hidden internal reasoning.

But they gave me something more useful than a retrospective story: a record produced with the change.

Evidence became practical: not certainty, but material that could be reviewed.

↓

---

## Post 8 — Reply to Post 7

This didn't begin as governance theory.

It began with three questions:

What should AI change?
Where should it stop?
What record lets us review the decision?

Scope. Boundary. Evidence.

That is governance emerging through engineering, not above it.
